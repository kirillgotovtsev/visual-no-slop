# Principles and verifiable criteria

These points help identify where form fails to serve the task. They are not a list of prohibited styles. Choose checks to suit the subject, genre and stage of work; consider the full set for a complete new composition and only the relevant points for a targeted change.

| Principle | Check and possible correction | Boundary and exception |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Subject specificity | Mentally substitute a different subject. If the expressive part loses nothing, identify which essential feature of the original subject is missing. Restore it in the image or composition. | Reusability is useful in a login form, table or catalog card. Universality is not a defect in itself. |
| 2. Image status | Compare what viewers are entitled to infer about a product, person, place or result with the source. Correct unsupported details or the way they are presented. | An artistic metaphor or a clearly identified concept need not be documentary. A missing source means uncertainty. |
| 3. The role of emphasis | Temporarily remove a strong effect and compare using the same content. Identify what was lost: understanding, character, mood, rhythm or recognizability. Remove or weaken anything that interferes with what is needed. | Atmosphere, a pause and the pleasure of form are legitimate roles. Minimalism is not required. |
| 4. Semantic hierarchy | Compare the intended order of attention with visible prominence. Strengthen an important distinction or reduce a competing emphasis. | A poster, artwork or editorial composition may reveal its meaning gradually. |
| 5. Real relationships and selective use of outlines | Read borders, lines, backgrounds and proximity as a system of relationships. Check whether elements of different importance have identical outlines and whether outlines duplicate an already clear separation. Compare with grouping through space, alignment or surfaces. Remove what is redundant while retaining necessary signals. | Repetition of equivalent entities helps comparison. A field outline, focus indicator, selection, axis or scale may be necessary. A thin line is not an error in itself. |
| 6. Coherence of the whole and its details | Check the details that carry meaning: contact points, connections, reflections, hands and tools, lettering, and perspective. Correct anything that violates the workings of the chosen world. | Surrealism, collage and deliberate distortion have their own logic. Photorealism is not a universal standard. |
| 7. Material character | Check light, surface, edge and scale against the task and the source. Preserve material features that matter for perception or identification. | Flat graphics, a deliberate plastic look and glossy stylization are legitimate. The number of effects does not measure quality. |
| 8. Typographic character and roles | Use actual headings, Cyrillic text, names and numbers to check letterforms, proportions, widths, weights and spacing. Relate visual character to the roles: where character is needed, where reading should be calm, and where comparison should be quick. Legible but uniformly neutral typesetting across all roles can leave a composition without character. Adjust the typesetting system, not only the family name. | An ordinary typeface may fit better than an unusual one. One family can be enough; two are not mandatory. Preserve expressive typesetting when it remains usable. A typeface's popularity does not establish its suitability in a particular place. |
| 9. Honest promises of action | Check the appearance of controls, progress, saving and success against the actual scenario. Correct the visual state or the real action within the authorized scope. | A static prototype may show a future state if its status is clear. Do not build a backend for a visual concept. |
| 10. Coherence across a series | Name the properties of the product, character, mark, color and behavior that should remain consistent. Compare angles, screens, media or frames and correct unintended differences. | A system allows variation; planned morphing or a change of angle does not amount to a loss of identity. |
| 11. Display conditions | Inspect the work at the required size, distance and aspect ratio, on the intended device and in the intended print or digital medium. Correct cropping and the loss of important information. | Do not demand every format from a draft for one medium. Accessibility and technical functionality matter but do not determine authorship. |
| 12. Content of diagrams and data | Recover the variables, units, categories, period, source and meaning of connections. Correct the visual encoding; do not fit the facts to the composition. | An abstract image of a topic may be decorative. It must not pass itself off as a factual chart. |
| 13. Suitability for the context | Check significant symbols and subject details against confirmed sources and for suitability to the audience. State uncertainty where expertise is needed. | An unfamiliar tradition, sentimentality and popular taste are not defects. Do not replace the audience's taste with your own. |
| 14. Considered selection | Compare variants using the same content, judging visual character, composition, hierarchy and result. Choose the one that serves the task and resolves the identified defect. | The number of variants, handmade roughness, a palette change and decorative asymmetry prove nothing on their own. |

## Examples of distinctions

- Three identical product cards with comparable specifications help people choose. Three equally emphasized blocks labeled "cause / result / action" may obscure their different roles; show exactly where this impedes reading.
- A gradient on a poster may support the character of an event. The same technique becomes a problem if it obscures a necessary caption in a particular work. Correct the loss of legibility.
- A cinematic product shot does not justify changing the construction of a real product. An atmospheric illustration of a fictional object does not need a catalog source.
- A figure of "98%" without a source requires a check of its meaning and status. Removing the number may require restructuring the block. Do not invent a source or a replacement number.
- Handmade collage, grain and an editorial grid can become as empty a template as a familiar technology landing page. Assess them on the same grounds.

- A neutral sans serif in a table may be exactly right. The same type treatment in the heading, price, buttons and advertising slogan may erase distinctions between roles. Show the relationships between sizes, widths and weights; do not stop at the typeface's name.
- A few thin dividers may organize data. A card border, a border around each row and a border around a nested button may repeat one boundary three times. Compare a version with fewer borders while keeping relationships, actions and focus distinguishable.
- A dark background, restrained sans serif and fine grid can form a convincing system or an interchangeable shell. Show how the techniques work together and relate to the subject; there is no need to establish AI provenance.

These are teaching examples, not facts about particular projects. Do not transfer their details into the user's material.
