# Checks by medium

Open the relevant section. Checks depend on the changes and the artifact's stage of completion; do not work through every section for every task.

## Interfaces and websites

Compare the main scenario with the hierarchy and the appearance of actions. Inspect actual text strings, images and data, along with the sizes and states relevant to the change. In a completed interface, check actions, focus and necessary feedback, as well as console and network errors that could affect the result. Use Chrome DevTools MCP when the project has it; preserve the user's tabs and close your own after recording the result. Do not infer functionality from a static screenshot.

Consider the expressive and functional roles of typography: a heading beside a product, numbers and specifications, and the density of captions. Check repeated pill shapes, borders and tiny labels. For lines, distinguish default, hover, selected and focused states: removing a decorative border must not remove feedback. Check whether space or a fill can replace a redundant border.

Cards, standard fields, system typography and familiar navigation can be sound choices. Assess empty "dashboards," false success states and a decorative slogan taking priority over necessary data against the specific scenario. An unfinished prototype does not require implementing the entire product.

## Identity, packaging, print and product design

Check the meaning of the visual code, typesetting in the actual language, preservation of necessary distinctions and behavior across the stated media. Inspect a mark at the required small size; for print and packaging, consider the scale and placement of mandatory information. For physical objects and 3D, compare construction, connections and materials with the sources and the stage of work. A mockup does not establish manufacturability, serve as a color proof or prove that the object exists.

## Posters, covers, editorial and document layouts

Assess the central visual idea, reading path and relationships between image and text. Check necessary information at the actual viewing distance or size; in a document, also inspect adjacent pages and the density of the real material. Preserve the intended rhythm and expressive typography. A technique need not explain everything immediately; it must serve the genre's purpose.

Check whether the typesetting shapes the visual character and distinguishes roles within the genre. Compare rules, borders, running headers and footers, and small labels: where they create rhythm and where they imitate editorial style without substance. Do not demand decorative asymmetry.

This section does not apply to work on text alone. Simply saving to another format does not authorize changes to the composition either.

## Images, illustrations and photographic scenes

Inspect the whole, then important contact points, connections, reflections, edges, lettering and subject details. Distinguish artistic convention from an error within the accepted visual language. For an image of a real product, compare significant properties with a confirmed source; for a series, compare identity across images. Do not replace missing catalog material with generated material presented as an original.

A prompt and a technical message saying "image created" do not replace inspection of the result. Correct raster images through an authorized image tool; keep editable vectors and layouts in their working format.

## Presentations, charts and infographics

Identify the takeaway of each content block. Check that scale, color, size and proximity support valid comparisons. In a chart, recover the variables, units, period and source; in a diagram, read connections as claims. Make sure labels are visible under the intended conditions. Check a slide deck for rhythm and consistency of rules; do not make every slide identical for the sake of order.

Compare the typographic roles of the takeaway, explanation and number. Check whether every block needs a border and whether repeating one layout obscures differences between ideas. Preserve lines that represent data and useful reading conventions.

Data analysis without creating or assessing a chart does not activate the skill. Attractive formatting does not make unsupported values acceptable.

## Video and animation

Check identity and causality over time, text legibility within the time available, the meaning of transitions and the direction of attention. Watch the motion in playback and inspect significant frames separately. A poster frame or contact sheet does not prove the quality of transitions. In an interface, check applicable motion constraints and accessible controls; in an artistic scene, assess deliberate metamorphosis against the intent.

## What counts as evidence

A screenshot, inspected export or frame establishes the appearance of the shown area under specific conditions. Interaction, video playback, a print proof and comparison with a source answer different questions. Name only checks that were actually performed. Where the project requires it, verify saving, reopening and the integrity of the editable file through its working tool.
