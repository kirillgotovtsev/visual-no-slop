# Visual patterns and counterexamples

These are prompts for careful inspection, not an authorship detector or a catalog of prohibited techniques. An observation concerns the visible composition and its task. Check both individual techniques and their accumulation. Resemblance to a template calls for analysis, not an automatic verdict.

## Neutral typography without a role

**What to look for.** One ordinary sans serif, similar styles and mechanical scaling serve the expressive heading, technical data and every action alike. There is no meaningful variation in density, proportions or rhythm. With the logo and image removed, the typesetting says nothing about the work's character.

**How to check.** Compare an actual large heading, short caption, long name and numbers. Name the missing quality: composure, scale, tension, calm or another character supported by the brief. Assess letter widths, Cyrillic letterforms, weight, letter and line spacing, line length and wrapping. Choose the family together with its typesetting rules. Keep the text, medium dimensions and image unchanged in the comparison.

**What to preserve.** Calm functional text, familiar numerals, accessibility and language coverage. Roboto, Inter, Arial and other common families are not prohibited; their familiarity makes checking their expressive role especially useful. One family with suitable styles may be enough. A rare typeface, a mandatory serif/sans pairing or another fashionable family does not automatically solve the problem.

## Thin borders in place of hierarchy

**What to look for.** Every block, row, panel and button receives the same thin border. Borders sit inside other borders; dividers repeat obvious gaps. Outlines give everything equal prominence, create a fine grid and distract from the text or image. Pale thin lines on a dark background can also create substantial visual noise in combination.

**How to check.** Classify visible lines by function: grouping, field, action, selection, focus, data or decoration. Within the authorized scope, remove a redundant outline and compare at the same size. Check whether proximity, spacing, alignment, typographic distinction or a surface is enough. The aim is a clear composition, not the fewest possible lines; replacing every border with a heavy panel can also make the result worse.

**What to preserve.** Keyboard focus, distinguishable selection, editable field boundaries, axes, ticks, technical diagrams and purposeful editorial rules. Do not remove them solely for "cleanliness." In an interactive medium, compare default, selected and focused states.

## A collection of ready-made techniques

**What to look for.** A combination of features rather than a single one: identical pill shapes, small uppercase labels, decorative numbering, thin dividers, neutral text and interchangeable product shots; or a cream background, a high-contrast serif and an unmotivated "editorial" look; or a black background, an acid accent and noise as a ready-made image of boldness. The list is open-ended: fashions and tool capabilities change.

**How to check.** Name the main compositional decision and the techniques that serve it. Which formal relationships follow from the subject? What remains formulaic? A convincing conclusion shows how the techniques interact in a specific place. There is no need to prove that each technique is bad on its own.

**What to preserve.** Useful conventions and an intentional genre. Design does not have to be unusual in every respect. Do not exchange one ready-made shell for another or add chaos and artificial "handmade" irregularity without a purpose.

Matching a list is not enough on its own. Look for an observable failure: the supporting grid has become more prominent than product differences; all actions receive equal emphasis; neutral letterforms and identical containers flatten the necessary distinction between an expressive heading and functional captions. A strong image can be the main technique, but the surrounding typesetting and structure still need this check.

## Repeated templates beyond interfaces

- In a presentation, check whether different ideas receive the same three-card layout and whether icons substitute for substantive graphics. Preserve repetition where it helps comparison.
- In a print layout, compare typesetting, rules, and running headers and footers with the genre and rhythm of the spreads. Small numbers, borders and pseudo-technical captions do not create editorial character on their own.
- In an image, assess more than hands and artifacts: generic staged lighting, uniformly smooth materials and arbitrary heroic treatment can strip the subject of specificity. Compare with the brief and source; stylization is legitimate.
- In motion, check whether the same showy entrance repeats across objects with different roles. Preserve transitions that help reveal causality and spatial relationships. A static frame does not replace this check.

These are teaching examples. Do not transfer their content, suggested character or conclusions into a project without checking.
